### Added
- **The GGUF tokenizer splits text as llama.cpp's `gpt-4o` pretokenizer
  (#243).** GPT-OSS (`tokenizer.ggml.pre = "gpt-4o"`, o200k_harmony) and
  Llama 4 (`"llama4"`, the same regex in llama.cpp) were encoded with the
  generic word splitter. A scanner now follows llama.cpp's
  `LLAMA_VOCAB_PRE_TYPE_GPT4O` regex: case runs ("CamelCase" splits,
  "HTTPServer" does not), contractions on the word, digits in threes,
  punctuation runs with a lead space and trailing newlines, and whitespace
  runs that leave their last codepoint to the next word, each fragment
  between special tokens on its own. Letter and number classes beyond ASCII
  come from llama.cpp's Unicode table (`src/engine/unicode_class.h`,
  generated by `tools/gen_unicode_classes.py`, 1540 ranges); a non-ASCII
  letter counts as upper and lower case at once, as in llama.cpp's
  collapsed regex. Against `llama-tokenize` at the bench pin on
  gpt-oss-20b's vocabulary: `tools/data/ppl_alice.txt` (5734 tokens), 14
  hand-made texts (code, numbers, contractions, mixed case, accents, Greek,
  Cyrillic, CJK, Arabic, Hebrew, Thai, Devanagari, emoji, combining marks,
  NBSP and em space, special tokens) and 150 random strings give identical
  ids. `test_gpt4o_pretok_unit` pins 26 chunkings.
