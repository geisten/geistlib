### Changed
- **`cpu_neon` runs Q5_0 on native W5A8 kernels: DeepSeek-V2-Lite's decode
  time falls by 21-27 % and its prefill time by 8-30 % on the Mac mini M6,
  and its prefill no longer depends on the chunking (#528).** Q5_0 is what llama-quantize
  leaves a K-quant model's tensor in when n_in is no multiple of 256:
  DeepSeek-V2-Lite Q4_K_M keeps 14 layers' `ffn_down_exps` (1408 -> 2048)
  in it. `cpu_neon` dequantized tiles of such a weight for the BLAS, sgemv
  for one row and sgemm for more. A BLAS picks its path by row count (one
  row goes through gemv, inside sgemm too), so a token routed to a Q5_0
  expert got other bits as the expert's only row of a prefill chunk than
  with company: with #527 in, the final logits at 64- against 128-row
  chunks on the M1 Max still differed (max |d| 0.93, rel L2 3.6 %). The
  new kernels unpack a block to 32 int8 in registers (no repack, no extra
  memory) and are the Q8_0 kernels from there, which give a row the same
  bits for every row count; DeepSeek-V2-Lite's final and teacher-forced
  logits are now bit-identical at both chunk sizes. The activations of
  these tensors become int8 with one scale per 32 elements, as for every
  other quantized format on this backend and as in llama.cpp's Q5_0 dot;
  they were fp32. Against `cpu_scalar` over 512 teacher-forced positions
  the distance does not grow: rel L2 0.0308 (main 0.0313), mean KL 0.0085
  nats (0.0088), top-1 agreement 479 / 512 (485). One expert-shape call
  with every thread busy on the M6: decode 502 -> 156 us, prefill of 8
  rows 1270 -> 320 us, of 32 rows 1450 -> 1268 us; from 64 rows the
  dequantized sgemm was faster (2236 -> 2540 us, 128 rows 3907 -> 4698
  us), which a model with a dense Q5_0 tensor would see. A fp32 GEMM of
  our own on the dequantized tile, the other option of #528, was measured
  and dropped: +4.3 % / +4.8 % on DeepSeek-V2-Lite's 128- / 512-token
  prefill.
